The AI Cash Starter Kit

A practical field guide for turning AI into leverage: find a problem, validate demand, shape the smallest useful offer, and create a real test.

Core rule: Do not ask, “How can AI make me money?” Ask, “What recurring problem can I help solve, and where can AI make the solution faster, clearer, cheaper, or easier to deliver?”

Part 2

25 reusable prompts

Replace the brackets. Ask the model to show assumptions and unknowns instead of pretending certainty.

Research • 1–5

1. Problem mapAct as a market researcher. For [audience], list 15 recurring problems connected to [domain]. Separate urgent problems, frequent problems, and expensive problems. For each, explain what evidence I should look for before assuming demand exists.
2. Buyer languageFor people dealing with [problem], generate the phrases they might use when searching Google, Reddit, YouTube, or asking a friend for help. Group by awareness level: symptom, problem, solution, and purchase intent.
3. Existing alternativesMap the current ways someone solves [problem] without my idea. Include software, freelancers, agencies, spreadsheets, manual work, ignoring the problem, and DIY methods. Explain the tradeoff of each.
4. Interview guideWrite 12 non-leading customer interview questions to learn how [audience] currently handles [problem]. Avoid pitching. Focus on past behavior, cost, frequency, workarounds, and what happens when the problem is not solved.
5. Evidence checklistCreate a validation checklist for [idea]. Divide evidence into weak signals, medium signals, and strong signals. Include searches, communities, existing spend, buyer interviews, preorders, and paid pilots.

Validation • 6–10

6. Assumption auditChallenge this idea: [idea]. List every assumption that must be true for it to work. Rank assumptions by risk and tell me the cheapest experiment to test each one.
7. Narrow the nicheTake this broad audience: [audience]. Create 10 narrower segments based on role, situation, urgency, or workflow. Do not rank them. For each, show what specific problem becomes more concrete.
8. Paid-pilot testDesign a 7-day paid-pilot test for [offer]. Keep delivery manual where possible. Define what I need to build, who I need to contact, what result to promise, and what evidence would tell me to continue, change, or stop.
9. Objection mapFor [offer] aimed at [audience], list likely objections around trust, price, effort, privacy, switching, quality, and timing. For each, suggest proof or product changes that could reduce the objection without hype.
10. Kill criteriaCreate objective kill criteria for testing [idea] over [time period]. Include demand, response rate, willingness to pay, delivery effort, and repeatability. I want permission to stop if the evidence is weak.

Offer • 11–15

11. Smallest useful offerTurn [problem] into the smallest useful paid offer I could deliver manually within 48 hours. Define input, process, output, scope boundary, turnaround time, and what is explicitly not included.
12. Outcome statementRewrite this offer in outcome-first language without earnings claims or exaggeration: [offer]. Give me 10 versions that say who it is for, what problem it handles, and what useful result is delivered.
13. Productize a serviceConvert this custom service into a repeatable package: [service]. Standardize the intake, deliverables, workflow, revision limit, turnaround time, and handoff so delivery becomes easier to repeat.
14. Price discovery questionsGive me customer interview questions that help uncover economic value for [problem] without directly asking “what would you pay?” Focus on time lost, current spend, frequency, risk, delays, and alternatives.
15. Proof planI have no testimonials yet for [offer]. Build an ethical proof plan using samples, before/after process examples, a pilot, measurable outputs, and transparent limitations. Do not fabricate social proof.

Content • 16–20

16. Hook bankCreate 20 hooks for [problem] aimed at [audience]. Use specificity, tension, curiosity, utility, or a surprising contrast. Avoid fake urgency, guaranteed outcomes, and “you won't believe” clickbait.
17. Teach the workflowTurn this workflow into a 20-second faceless Reel: [workflow]. Structure: 2-second hook, 3 visual beats, one practical takeaway, soft CTA. Keep the language conversational and specific.
18. CarouselWrite a 9-slide Instagram carousel about [problem]. Slide 1 = clear hook. Slide 2 = why the default approach fails. Slides 3-6 = tactical steps. Slide 7 = example. Slide 8 = checklist. Slide 9 = save/share CTA.
19. Repurpose winnerThis post performed well: [paste post]. Extract the core idea, not the wording. Create 5 new executions: contrarian, tutorial, case example, checklist, and myth-vs-reality.
20. CTA ladderFor [content topic], write 3 CTAs: one for engagement, one for lead capture, and one for a paid offer. Keep each natural and low-pressure.

Operations • 21–25

21. SOP draftTurn these notes into an SOP: [notes]. Include purpose, trigger, inputs, steps, quality checks, exceptions, owner, and definition of done. Flag anything ambiguous instead of inventing details.
22. Quality checklistBuild a pre-delivery quality checklist for [deliverable]. Include factual accuracy, privacy, formatting, completeness, human review, broken links, and client-specific requirements.
23. Automation candidate auditReview this workflow: [workflow]. Classify each step as keep human, assist with AI, automate with rules, or do not automate. Explain the risk of automating each step.
24. Weekly reviewHere are this week's metrics: [data]. Separate observations from interpretations. Identify what changed, what might explain it, what is still uncertain, and the one variable I should test next week.
25. Decision memoHelp me decide whether to continue [idea]. Use this evidence: [evidence]. Produce: facts, assumptions, strongest case for continuing, strongest case for stopping, next cheapest test, and a decision deadline. Do not make up missing evidence.
Part 3

The AI Cash Scorecard

Score each dimension 0, 1, or 2. Do not inflate the score because you like the idea.

Pain urgency

How costly or frustrating is the problem right now?

0 Mild1 Real2 Urgent

Existing spend

Do people already pay money, time, or staff effort to solve it?

0 None1 Some2 Clear

Frequency

Does the problem happen often enough to matter?

0 Rare1 Periodic2 Frequent

AI leverage

Can AI materially improve speed, research, drafting, analysis, or consistency?

0 Little1 Helpful2 Strong

Delivery simplicity

Can you deliver a useful first version without building a full platform?

0 Hard1 Possible2 Simple

Buyer reachability

Can you identify and contact people who experience the problem?

0 Hard1 Some2 Easy
Interpretation: 9–12 = worth a real test. 6–8 = refine the segment or offer before building. 0–5 = gather more evidence or move on. A score is a hypothesis, not proof.
Part 4

Your 7-day action plan

The goal is not to “launch a business” in seven days. The goal is to leave the week with better evidence than you started with.

DAY 1

Problem map

Choose one audience. List recurring problems. Pick three to investigate.

DAY 2

Evidence hunt

Find buyer language, current alternatives, complaints, and signs of existing spend.

DAY 3

Talk to people

Run 3–5 short conversations. Ask about past behavior, not hypothetical interest.

DAY 4

Shape the offer

Define one narrow input, process, output, boundary, and turnaround time.

DAY 5

Build a sample

Create one realistic example or manual pilot. Do not overbuild infrastructure.

DAY 6

Distribute

Send targeted outreach or publish useful content that exposes the problem and workflow.

DAY 7

Review evidence

Score the idea again. Continue, change one variable, or stop without guilt.

Build from evidence, not excitement.
